## How have I used AI for coding so far?

Mostly I paste the whole problem into ChatGPT when I get stuck after like several minutes of trials. Sometimes I ask it to explain error messages too.


## Do I usually try problems myself first?

Not always. If it looks scary and or difficult, I just go straight to AI. But lately I’ve been trying longer — maybe a few minutes now.


## Can I explain code I’ve submitted without AI’s help?

Since I'm a beginner coder, for easy stuff I'd say yes. But anything with recursion, classes, or two-pointer tricks — I usually just copy and try to understand it line by line and hope the interviewer doesn’t ask me to walk through it.


## What would happen if AI was unavailable during an exam or job test?

I’d probably only solve the easy ones and struggle with others as much as I could. In fact I encountered it several times during my piscine-go.

## Current pattern:
Right now I’m 80% Learner A (answer generator) and 20% Learner B (learning amplifier).

## What I want to become:

I want to be mostly Learner B. Use AI like a really smart tutor - only after I’ve tried hard, and mainly to understand why something works or what I missed. I don’t want to be the guy who can’t code without Copilot.

Today I mostly treat AI like Google on steroids - quick answers, fast progress, but sometimes I forget almost everything in a short while. I want to change that. I want to use AI to make me actually smarter, not just faster. If I keep depending on it to think for me, I’ll be useless the second AI gets restricted or I’m in an interview without my laptop. Hence, it's time to struggle more now so I don’t suffer later.
