## Interview:

If I get asked to design a caching system and I’ve always just asked AI to “write me an LRU cache”, I’ll probably be in a difficult position at that moment. Without understanding the trade-offs, like why hashmap + linkedlist is often the go-to, or what I'd sacrifice with other approaches. I’d sound like I’m reciting, not thinking.


## Production Bug at 2 AM:

If AI is down and I have to debug code I copied months ago, I may find it difficult - particularly if I haven't taken time to understand the same code line by line. I won’t know what invariants were supposed to hold or why certain checks exist.


## New Technology:

If a brand new library comes out; say, some hot new Golang or Rust web framework, and no AI knows it yet, I’d have to read the actual docs and write small experiments in order to get acquainted with it. If I don't practice doing that, I’d be very slow and frustrated.


## Reflection:

I'm using AI the fair way now - trying hard first, reflecting, only asking targeted questions. It is like a weight training for my brain. When real pressure comes: no internet, new tech, interviewers watching;  I won’t panic because I already built the habit of thinking for myself. Shortcuts may feel good today but they could leave me weak tomorrow. So I’d rather be slow and strong than fast and fragile.
