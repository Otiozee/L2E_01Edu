# Exercise 2: Designing Ethical Prompts

## Part A – My independent bad prompts

### Bad prompt 1:
"Write a story about a successful engineer"
- Problem: Might default to male/white/young.
- Harm: Reinforces stereotypes.
- Assumption: Engineer = certain look.

### Improved prompt 1:
"Write a diverse story about a successful engineer - include people of different genders, ages, ethnic backgrounds, and abilities. Make sure no stereotypes."
- Why better: Forces diversity, removes hidden bias.

### Bad prompt 2: 
"Give medical advice for headache"
- Problem: AI isn't doctor, could give wrong/dangerous info.
- Harm: Someone skips real help.
- Assumption: Safe to give medical advice.

### Improved prompt 2:
"I'm not a doctor - do not give medical advice. Instead, list common causes of headaches from reliable sources like professional Clinics and say to see a professional."
- Why its better: Allow AI to give advice based on available resources

### Bad prompt 3:
"Describe a typical software developer"
- Problem: Might default to male/white/young.
- Harm: Reinforces stereotypes.
- Assumption: developer = certain look.

### Improved prompt 3:
"Describe a range of real software developers from different countries, backgrounds, and lifestyles based on actual diversity stats."
- Why better: Forces diversity, removes hidden bias.

## Part B – Tested original vs improved in ChatGPT/Claude.
- Originals gave stereotypical outputs sometimes.
- Improved ones were way more neutral/diverse/safe. Surprised how much just adding "diverse" and "no stereotypes" changed it.
- Asked AI to critique my improved: It said good, but watch for over-correction (forced diversity can feel unnatural). Also add "base on facts" to avoid made-up stats.
